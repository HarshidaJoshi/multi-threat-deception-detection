Please consider our original article, “How Far Do Explainable Detectors of Digital Deception
Generalise? A Cross-Dataset Evaluation of an Intelligent Multi-Module System,” for publication
in *Cybernetics and Systems*.

The manuscript is relevant to the journal’s coverage of applied artificial intelligence,
intelligent systems, human--machine interaction, and decision-making under uncertainty. We study
an explainable multi-module system for synthetic-image, fake-news, and recruitment-fraud
detection. Rather than relying on in-domain accuracy alone, we evaluate how each module responds
when its operating environment changes across image generators, news corpora, and employers.
The study includes confidence intervals, paired significance tests, repeated splits, grouped
validation, and a controlled mixed-source image experiment.

The central result is that high in-domain performance is not a reliable measure of operational
behaviour. A synthetic-image classifier reaches 95.4% accuracy in-domain but approaches chance
on other image sources; article-level news classifiers deteriorate on short claims; and a
recruitment classifier’s F1 falls from 0.85 under random splitting to 0.60 when employers are
held out. Mixing two known image sources repairs transfer between those sources but not to an
independently collected third set. Explanations are computed from the models themselves using
Grad-CAM and exact linear feature contributions, supporting auditable human review.
